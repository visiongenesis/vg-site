The idea

A grandmother emailed you.

She’s raising her granddaughter. The girl wrestles. And the week was simply out of reach — a combination of cost and logistics. I watched that exchange come through while I was working your funnel, and I kept thinking about the economic reality for a number of families I know on our own team at Halls — a very blue-collar Knoxville community — and the fact that those families will only ever see your price list as a wall.

Gold Medal Mat Scholars is the answer I want to build with you. Area high school coaches nominate their hardest-working wrestlers. Local Knoxville businesses put up $475 per wrestler. Your camp matches every dollar in tuition. Vision Genesis runs the entire campaign — coaches, sponsors, press — and drives the vans to Hamilton on July 18.

No applications, no hardship forms, no charity label. A coach’s award, a sponsor’s name, and a kid on the mat with an Olympian.

The unit — matched dollar-for-dollar

The family pays nothing. Sponsor money goes straight to your registration — we never hold a dollar.

And I’ve got skin in it.

Vision Genesis sponsors every kid’s transportation, fuel, and shirts — up to $1,500 cash out of my own pocket — on top of running a campaign that would bill north of $10,000 if you hired it out. Your side costs no cash at all. I’m putting real money on these kids; your match makes us partners in it.

The temptation is to read ten sponsored wrestlers as money left on the table. The real ledger: at zero cash cost to you — rooms aren’t your constraint in Hamilton — you’ve funded an organized, well-publicized, two-market marketing campaign. This is not one token scholarship. Twenty years ago you had a Knoxville market; this reopens it.

Press in both markets — handled.

We coordinate Knoxville and Cincinnati press: releases written, outlets pitched, the send-off staged. The goal is real, professional local news coverage — not a Facebook post. You just need to be available, and lend a hand on filming logistics at the Ohio end.

A story engine — and a refreshed list.

Every sponsored wrestler puts your camp inside a team group chat that’s never heard it, with the head coach as your advocate — 8–14 new coach relationships in one cycle. And every family, ride-along, and sponsor is a fresh East Tennessee email into your list: a warm rebuild of an old market, plus a summer of footage for your ad team.
